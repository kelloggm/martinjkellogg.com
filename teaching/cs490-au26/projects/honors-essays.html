Honors Reflection Essays

These are individual assignments, and they are required for students in the Honors section of this course (see the Honors section of the syllabus). Students who are not in the Honors section do not need to do them.

Twice during the semester, you must write a short reflection essay about one of the “Your Choice” readings:

  • Essay 1: Due Friday 9 October 2026, 11:59pm AoE, i.e., before the first mid-term exam.
  • Essay 2: Due Sunday 13 December 2026, 11:59pm AoE, i.e., before the final exam.

Both essays have the same requirements and the same rubric, which are described below.

Learning Goals

Read a paper about software engineering in depth, try out one of its ideas yourself, and reflect on the experience.

The Task

Write a 500-word essay about one of the “Your Choice” readings on the course calendar.

For Essay 1, you must choose a reading that was assigned on or before October 7 (that is, any “Your Choice” reading assigned before the essay is due). For Essay 2, you may choose any other “Your Choice” reading from the semester. The two essays must be about two different readings.

There is no sign-up sheet, and there is no limit on the number of students who may write about a given reading: choose whichever reading interests you most. You are also welcome to write about one of the two “Your Choice” readings that you are relying on to answer the “Your Choice” question on the exams (see the “Your Choice” readings page), though writing one of these essays requires you to engage with the reading much more deeply than the exam question will.

You should start by reading your chosen article in detail. Then, you should try out something discussed in the reading yourself. For example, if the article is about a particular code review technique, you should spend some time actually reviewing a change to an existing codebase using the proposed technique; if the article is about an estimation method, you should try to produce (and then check!) an estimate of your own using that method; etc. Your group project is a convenient place to try things out, but you are not required to use it. It’s up to you exactly how you interpret this requirement, but your essay should clearly explain what you did and why.

Write your essay.

Good essays have concrete examples, e.g., it did x but I expected y in the context of z. Bad essays are vague and employ generic sentiments, like “I like it!”, or “It’s helpful.”.

Be sure to write down your name, UCID, and the title of the reading that you chose at the top of the page.

AI Constraints

You should not use any AI, GenAI, or LLM to write these essays. You may use any tool you wish to proofread your essay for grammar, but do not use any tool that creates or changes the meaning of what you have written.

Note: You will be on the hook for understanding and defending the opinions that you write in these reflection essays. You must understand and buy into the contents of your essay at the time you turn it in, because the course staff may ask you about it afterwards (including on an exam!).

Submission

Submit each essay as a PDF file to Canvas, under the assignments “Honors Reflection Essay 1” and “Honors Reflection Essay 2”.

If you prepare the essay in some other software (like Tex, Word, or Google Docs), please export it as PDF before submitting.

Rubric

Each essay is worth 2% of your project grade, as one of the project’s individually-graded components: see the project overview for how the project grade fits together. Because Honors students write these two essays in addition to everything else, the individually-graded portion of an Honors student’s project grade is 13% rather than 9%.

Each essay is graded out of 100 points. The points are allocated for the following:

  1. Name, UCID, and the title of your chosen reading are written at the top of the page: 5 pts.
  2. Essay is between 450-550 words: 10 pts.
  3. Essay shows that you have read and reflected on the reading: 30 pts.
  4. Essay clearly describes what you did to try out the reading’s ideas, and why: 30 pts.
  5. Essay contains meaningful reflections that derive from your personal experience: 25 pts.
